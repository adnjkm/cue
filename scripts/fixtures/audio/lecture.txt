Okay everyone, let's get started. Today we are continuing with techniques of integration, and the main topic is integration by parts. If you remember the product rule for derivatives, integration by parts is really just the product rule run backwards. The formula is: the integral of u d v equals u times v, minus the integral of v d u. The whole trick is choosing u and d v well. A good rule of thumb is LIATE: logarithms, inverse trig, algebraic, trig, and exponential. Whatever comes first in that list is usually your u. Let's do an example. Take the integral of x times e to the x, d x. Here x is algebraic and e to the x is exponential, so we let u equal x and d v equal e to the x d x. Then d u is d x and v is e to the x. Plugging in, we get x e to the x, minus the integral of e to the x, which is x e to the x minus e to the x, plus C. Now, pay attention, because this will be on Term Test 2. You will get at least one question where you have to apply integration by parts twice, like the integral of x squared sine x.
